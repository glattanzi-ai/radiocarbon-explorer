Reference

Marine reservoir (ΔR) corrections

Marine organisms exchange carbon with dissolved inorganic carbon in the ocean, which is depleted in ¹⁴C relative to the atmosphere because of slow mixing with deep water. This global offset — roughly 400 ¹⁴C years on average — is already built into the Marine20 curve. Local currents, upwelling, and freshwater input cause additional site-specific deviation from that global average, captured by a regional correction term, ΔR.

X_adj = X_measured − ΔR   |   σ_adj = √(σ_measured² + σ_ΔR²)

The adjusted age and its inflated uncertainty are then calibrated against Marine20 exactly as a normal date is calibrated against IntCal20 or SHCal20. A positive ΔR means the local reservoir is older than the global average (push the effective age younger before calibration); a negative ΔR means it is younger.

Regional ΔR values are compiled from paired known-age shell measurements in the Marine Reservoir Correction Database (Reimer & Reimer, calib.org). The presets below cover the Chesapeake Bay / Middle Atlantic coast, directly relevant to the I-5247 (Loyola Retreat, Potomac River) example.

Methodology

For every date, the lab-reported Gaussian likelihood N(BP, σ) is compared against the calibration curve's own age and uncertainty at each calendar year, following the standard probabilistic approach used by CALIB and OxCal (Stuiver & Reimer 1993; Bronk Ramsey 2009). The resulting density is normalized so its integral over calendar time equals 1, then the narrowest region(s) containing 68.3% (1σ) and 95.4% (2σ) of that probability are reported as highest-posterior-density (HPD) ranges — these can be disjoint where the calibration curve has plateaus or reversals ("wiggles"). Median calendar ages are the 50th-percentile of the cumulative distribution. All computation runs locally in your browser.
